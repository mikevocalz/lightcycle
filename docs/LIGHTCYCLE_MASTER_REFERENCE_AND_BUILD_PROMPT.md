# TRON LIGHT CYCLE — MASTER REFERENCE + BUILD PROMPT

**Target:** a realistic current-generation AAA / PS5-style Light Cycle that blends the **1982 Syd Mead DNA** with the **mechanical realism and physicality of TRON: Ares**.

**Core rule:** this is **one canonical modular bike**, not six separate color variants. Blue, red, gold, purple, green, and white are runtime-configurable energy identities.

---

## 1. VISUAL TARGET

The bike must feel like a production hero vehicle from a premium current-generation console game:

- photoreal / grounded sci-fi
- dense hard-surface construction
- believable scale and mass
- physically based materials
- realistic panel gaps, fasteners, joints, bearings, brakes, pivots, housings and service access
- real material separation between composite, metal, rubber, glass and emissive surfaces
- restrained wear and micro-surface variation
- cinematic HDR lighting and reflections
- realistic rider ergonomics
- enough detail for extreme closeups

Do **not** make it cartoonish, cel-shaded, anime-styled, toy-like, mobile-game-like, or like a normal superbike with neon pasted over it.

### Design formula

**1982 silhouette + Ares engineering + AAA realism**

The silhouette should carry the 1982 design language from far away:

- two enormous circular wheel masses
- very low stance
- long horizontal proportion
- rider almost inserted into the machine
- minimal conventional motorcycle anatomy
- strong clean shapes at distance

The close-up engineering should pull from Ares:

- milled aluminum / precision metal
- visible structural logic
- hubless-wheel-like mechanical complexity
- articulated armor and protection
- central energy / gyro assembly
- real joints and pivots
- believable construction and maintenance access

# 3. NON-CARTOON / PS5 REALISM OVERRIDE

The final bike must survive this test:

> **If all emissive lighting is switched off, does the bike still look like an exceptionally detailed, believable AAA science-fiction vehicle?**

If no, the asset is relying too heavily on neon.

## Physical materials

### Composite body
- satin and gloss black composite
- selective painted carbon
- restrained exposed carbon weave where structurally sensible
- micro scratches in clear coat
- subtle fingerprints / smudging only where plausible
- material-to-material roughness variation
- no flat black plastic look

### Metal
- CNC-machined aluminum
- dark anodized aluminum
- brushed / bead-blasted precision metal
- blackened steel
- high-wear bearing surfaces
- realistic fasteners
- subtle machining direction / anisotropy where appropriate

### Wheel contact surfaces
- believable high-performance synthetic tire/contact material
- mild thermal and abrasion variation
- small roughness changes on the contact patch
- no pure smooth neon discs

### Glass / translucent materials
- smoked technical lens material
- physically plausible thickness
- edge reflections
- emissive elements can sit behind translucent housings where appropriate

# 4. PLAYER COLOR SYSTEM — ONE MODEL, SIX IDENTITIES

Create **one `lightcycle.glb`**.

Do not create six separate GLBs.

Allowed runtime player energy choices:

| Color | Energy | Hot Core | Halo |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blue | `#00C8FF` | `#E7FCFF` | `#008CFF` |
| Red | `#FF291A` | `#FFF0E8` | `#FF0037` |
| Gold | `#FFC13D` | `#FFF3BD` | `#FF8700` |
| Purple | `#B65CFF` | `#F3E5FF` | `#7900FF` |
| Green | `#31FF83` | `#E7FFF0` | `#00C95B` |
| White | `#EAF8FF` | `#FFFFFF` | `#A7DFFF` |

These are **game-production targets**, not claims of exact film color values.

### Energy rendering model

Each glow should read as:

1. white-hot / near-white core
2. saturated player energy body
3. controlled HDR halo / bloom
4. colored reflection on nearby physical materials

Never render the whole bike as a flat colored emissive object.

The body remains mostly graphite/black/metal. **Energy is the player's identity.**

# 5. LIGHTING DESIGN

Target approximately:

- **70% broad graphic luminous fields** inspired by 1982
- **30% precision technical strips/details** inspired by modern TRON / Ares

Do not outline every body panel.

Energy should visually flow through a plausible system:

`front wheel -> front conductor -> central reactor -> body energy bus -> rear wheel -> trail emitter`

The energy system must control:

- front wheel ring
- rear wheel ring
- reactor
- body primary emitter
- body secondary emitter
- cockpit status light
- boost effect
- light ribbon / wall
- collision flash
- damage flicker
- derezz effect
- Rive player color identity
- multiplayer HUD / scoreboard accent

# 7. TRANSFORMING 1982-STYLE HIGH-SPEED SILHOUETTE

Keep the rider more exposed in normal / combat mode.

In boost / high-speed mode:

- rear architecture moves forward/down
- protection closes around the rider
- the rider visually sinks into the chassis
- the overall silhouette becomes closer to the monolithic 1982 Light Cycle

Transformation must be done with real mechanical movement:

- pivots
- hinges
- sliding rails
- articulated supports
- telescoping pieces where justified

Do not scale the body or fake the whole transformation with a global morph.

---

# 8. CENTRAL REACTOR / GYRO — HERO DETAIL

The reactor must be one of the model's hero systems, not a decorative glowing disc.

Use:

- nested precision rings
- bearings
- moving aperture / shield pieces
- counter-rotation
- suspended central energy core
- articulated structural yoke
- visible energy transfer conduits

State behavior:

### Idle
- slow mechanical motion
- low pulse
- subtle reflections on nearby metal

### Acceleration
- increased ring speed
- more energetic pulse
- brighter conduits

### Boost
- high-speed counter rotation
- concentrated white-hot center
- strong but controlled bloom
- visible energy travel toward wheels and trail emitter

### Damage
- unstable rotation
- intermittent energy dropout
- sparks / arcs
- localized flicker

### Destruction
- overload
- loss of synchronization
- final derezz / breakup sequence

# 10. DAMAGE / DESTRUCTION

Do not require a totally separate destroyed model.

Use the modular structure for:

- panel detach
- localized deformation where supported
- damage flicker
- reactor instability
- sparks
- broken / exposed internal structures
- wheel shutdown
- final derezz fragmentation

The collision system should use simple invisible gameplay proxies, not the full render mesh:

```text
COL_LC_Body
COL_LC_FrontWheel
COL_LC_RearWheel
COL_LC_RiderZone
```

# 11. RUNTIME MATERIAL ARCHITECTURE

The canonical model has neutral PBR materials and neutral grayscale emissive masks.

Recommended material names:

```text
MAT_LC_GRAPHITE
MAT_LC_BLACK_CLEARCOAT
MAT_LC_MILLED_METAL
MAT_LC_BRUSHED_METAL
MAT_LC_RUBBER
MAT_LC_SMOKED_GLASS
MAT_LC_CARBON
MAT_LC_EMISSIVE_PRIMARY
MAT_LC_EMISSIVE_CORE
MAT_LC_EMISSIVE_REACTOR
MAT_LC_EMISSIVE_SECONDARY
```

Do not bake player hue into the texture.

Three.js and Viro should receive the same semantic config:

```ts
type LightCycleEnergyConfig = {
  energyColor: string
  coreColor: string
  haloColor: string
  energyIntensity: number
  coreIntensity: number
  bloomStrength: number
}
```

Then create renderer adapters:

```text
ThreeLightCycleMaterialAdapter
ViroLightCycleMaterialAdapter
```

The goal is visual equivalence, not identical renderer numbers.

---

# 12. MULTIPLAYER / INSTANCE-SAFE COLOR

Player 1 and Player 2 must be able to use different colors while sharing the same geometry and textures.

Do **not** mutate globally shared material instances.

Share:

- geometry
- non-color PBR textures
- normal maps
- metallic / roughness / AO
- emissive masks
- animation clips

Per player instance, clone or parameterize only the material state that actually changes.

Example:

```text
Player 1: MIKE — BLUE
Player 2: ALEX — GOLD
```

Both use the same `lightcycle.glb`.

# 13. ZUSTAND — NO useState

Use the project's Zustand architecture as the single source of truth.

Do not introduce React `useState` for Light Cycle gameplay/customization state.

Example semantic state:

```ts
playerId
playerName
cycleColor
cycleState
speed
boost
health
isDestroyed
```

Changing `cycleColor` must propagate to:

- bike materials
- reactor
- wheels
- trail
- boost FX
- collision FX
- derezz FX
- Rive HUD
- leaderboard
- player badge

# 14. LIGHT RIBBON / TRAIL

The trail is runtime geometry/effect, not permanently included in the GLB.

Use `LC_FX_TrailOrigin` as the stable emission transform.

Trail visual structure:

- white-hot leading edge
- saturated player color
- translucent energetic body
- controlled bloom
- slight distortion / heat-energy shimmer

Must work simultaneously for all six player colors.

---

# 15. RIDER ERGONOMICS

The rider must physically fit and be supported by the machine.

Target posture:

- torso near horizontal
- shoulders low
- elbows and wrists naturally reaching controls
- chest supported
- knees located in intended recesses
- shins and feet mechanically supported
- no mesh intersection
- no floating rider

The bike should look large enough that the rider feels integrated into a serious machine rather than sitting on a normal motorcycle.

Primary Ares ergonomics research:
- https://julianbell.ca/tron-ares

---

# 16. AAA MODELING / LOD TARGETS

Create a high-poly source first for clean baking and hero renders.

Starting triangle targets — profile and tune per platform rather than treating these as absolute:

- **Cinematic/source:** unrestricted high-poly source
- **LOD0 hero:** ~180k-300k tris
- **LOD1 gameplay:** ~90k-150k tris
- **LOD2 distant:** ~40k-70k tris
- **LOD3 very distant:** ~15k-30k tris

Hero texture sets can use 4K where justified.

Runtime targets should use efficient texture compression such as KTX2/Basis where supported.

# 18. FINAL QUALITY GATE

Do not approve the asset until all are true:

- silhouette unmistakably reads as a Light Cycle
- does not look like a normal superbike
- 1982 DNA visible from long distance
- Ares-level construction logic visible close up
- physical materials look expensive with emissions disabled
- rider fits properly
- model remains modular
- wheels and reactor animate independently
- high-speed transformation uses real pivots
- damage components are separate
- one GLB supports all six colors
- runtime color switching does not reload the GLB
- player instances do not recolor one another
- trail is runtime-generated
- Three.js and Viro retain the same semantic part names
- no `useState`; Zustand only
- asset remains viable for Quest/Pico/other XR via LOD and performance profiling
